# Coontie product spec

Status: v0.1, 2026-10-21. Derived from the rules (`docs/requirements/`), the pain
research (`~/.hermes/workspace/coontie-recon/PAIN-POINTS-2026-10-21.md`), and the
rulings in `DECISIONS.md`. Nothing here is certified or deployed.

---

## 1. One sentence

Coontie is a free, MIT-licensed system of record for a Florida MMTC's internal
seed-to-sale obligations: it holds the record the state requires, makes corrections
safe, shows where the record has drifted, and produces the state's own documents on
demand.

Not a point of sale. Not an e-commerce storefront. Not a reporting product. The
record is the product.

## 2. Who it is for

**Primary:** the 22 new licensees (MMTC-2026-0029 through -0050). They have no
incumbent to displace, they must integrate before cultivation authorization, and they
are building right now.

**Secondary:** the 27 existing operators, for whom "replacement" is the frame and the
cutover kit is the product.

**The person who feels it:** the compliance lead or data systems manager. In the rule
workshop record, these people are named, present, and specific about their pain. They
are not buyers of features. They are buyers of "I do not have to call the state's
vendor to fix my own mistake."

## 3. What the research changed

The recon moved three things and killed one.

| Finding from the research | What it changed in the build |
|---|---|
| "Correcting an error takes days or weeks and a vendor relationship" (Senne, OMMU workshop); "you can't really move your inventory until that's done" | **Corrections become the product's center, not a settings screen.** Every record is amendable with a reason, and the justification the department requires is generated by the same action |
| "These two don't sync at times and then it breaks... you need to see where the drift happened" (r/weedbiz); "10 systems... not designed to work together" (FL dispensary staff) | **A drift view against the state record is a first-class surface**, not an analytics afterthought |
| "There is zero customer support... tickets that never get resolved"; "abandoned after onboarding" (r/weedbiz) | **Onboarding must be self-service and days, not a three-day install.** A guided first run that ingests the operator's own export is the entry point |
| Every vendor competes on menus, e-commerce, loyalty and AI recommendations; operators describe those as the parts that break | **None of those are in scope, ever.** They are the loud market and the wrong product |

The research did **not** change: the module order (inventory core, then cultivation,
processing, transport, and dispensing last), the DH5059 schema floor, the MIT licence,
or the neutral brand.

## 4. The KISS contract

Seven rules that decide every feature argument. A feature that breaks one of these
does not ship.

1. **One screen per job.** If a task takes more than one screen, the task was not
   designed yet.
2. **The record is the product.** Any feature that only makes the record prettier is
   cosmetic and goes last or never.
3. **Corrections are first class.** Nothing is deleted. A wrong weight is amended,
   attributed, and justified, and the ledger shows both the error and the fix.
4. **Drift is shown, never hidden.** If the state's copy and ours disagree, the
   disagreement is visible with a name on which side is wrong.
5. **No paywalled basics.** Exports, reports and history are free and always available.
6. **Days, not months.** An operator with an export and a license number stands up a
   working record in one session.
7. **No contract, no seat count, no lock-in.** MIT, local-first, and the data leaves
   in the state's own field names whenever asked.

## 5. The five flows that are the product

Everything else is support for these five.

### Flow 1: First run into a working record

- **Actor:** compliance lead at a new licensee, or an operator leaving an incumbent.
- **Steps:** facility and rooms, products with unique product IDs (the six-attribute
  rule), import an incumbent export or start empty, first inventory list, export the
  DH5058 inventory list for the integration plan.
- **Rules enforced:** 64-4.221(1)(c) and (5)(a) (unique product ID assignment), 64-4.222(11)
  (precise location), DH5059 field shapes.
- **Done when:** the operator has a record, a DH5058-shaped inventory list, and a
  checklist for the integration plan, without a support call.

### Flow 2: Record the day

- **Actor:** cultivation, processing and inventory staff.
- **Steps:** the DH5059 event set, in the order work happens: seed and clone batches,
  plants, additives, harvest scheduling and harvest, cure and dry, lots and splits,
  conversions, ingredients, final products, lab samples, delivery devices, room and
  plant moves, disposals.
- **Rules enforced:** the full 33-event catalog, 12-hour timing, 72-hour disposal wait,
  immutable expiration.
- **Done when:** every event in the catalog can be recorded from a single screen with
  the fields the form requires, and the ledger's hash chain verifies.

### Flow 3: Correct without fear

- **Actor:** compliance lead, after the mistake is discovered.
- **Steps:** find the record, state what happened, choose a cause, amend. If the cause
  is not processing, dispensation, wholesale or waste disposal, Coontie drafts the
  written justification for `ommu_seedtosale@flhealth.gov` and starts the 12-hour clock.
- **Rules enforced:** 64-4.222(12) and the five exempt causes; the append-only ledger
  keeps the original and the amendment.
- **Done when:** no correction is possible without a cause, and every non-exempt
  correction produces a justification draft and a deadline.

### Flow 4: See the drift

- **Actor:** compliance lead, weekly, or after any failure.
- **Steps:** import the state record, compare to ours, classify each difference
  (missing here, missing there, quantity mismatch, status mismatch), fix from the
  difference row.
- **Rules enforced:** 64-4.222(2) and (3) indirectly; the drift itself is the signal.
- **Done when:** the operator can answer "is my record the same as the state's" in one
  screen, with a number and a list.

### Flow 5: Answer the state, on demand

- **Actor:** anyone asked by the department.
- **Steps:** native CSV of the whole ledger, an itemized outage report, a manifest, an
  evidence pack for one inventory item's history.
- **Rules enforced:** 64-4.222(4) (48 hours), 64-4.222(2)(b) (24-hour outage CSV).
- **Done when:** each of those documents is one action, carries the state's field
  names, and is reproducible byte for byte.

## 6. Guardrails that must be impossible to miss

From the research, the failures that hurt are the ones nobody noticed.

- A package or lot on hold that can still be sold.
- A product near expiry that nobody is watching.
- An adjustment sitting without its justification.
- A disconnection clock running past two hours.
- A record whose location is stale.

Each of these is a visible, count-bearing item on the main screen. No dashboards, no
charts. A number, a list, and a fix action.

## 7. What we refuse

- E-commerce, menus, menu boards, online ordering.
- Loyalty, marketing, SMS campaigns.
- Payments and cash management.
- AI product recommendations.
- Reporting products and paywalled tiers.
- Any third-party reader of the record (64-4.222(5) makes that disciplinable).
- An offline dispensing mode (64-4.222(2)(a): a degraded mode there is a violation).

## 8. Shape of the build

Local-first, single box, SQLite, no cloud dependency, no account required. The record
lives with the operator. Export is a first-class action, not a feature flag.

| Layer | What it is | State |
|---|---|---|
| Rules | 22 executable checks with citations | built |
| Event catalog | 33 DH5059 records with fields and timing | built |
| Ledger | append-only, hash-chained, native CSV | built |
| Domain model | the entities the catalog forces | built |
| First run | guided setup plus incumbent import | **built (S1)**: CLI first run, CSV import, DH5058 export |
| Corrections | amend with cause and justification drafting | **built (S2)**: amend, justifications, justify, journal, alerts |
| Drift | comparison against the state record | **built (S4)**: pull the department's export, classify, resolve on a one-screen page |
| Guardrails | hold, expiry, adjustment, disconnect clock | **built (S3)**: the clock, the notice, the restoration notice, the outage CSV |
| Cultivation | plants, harvests, waste, moves | **built (S5)**: integration gate, batches, plants, moves, additives, schedule-then-harvest, 72h disposal |
| Processing | lots, splits, conversions, final products, lab samples | **built (S6)**: cure, lots, splits, conversions, ingredients, samples, final products with immutable expiration |
| Transport | manifests and transfers | **built (S7)**: vehicles, manifest with its printable page, dispatch, receipt with rejections, wholesale invoice |
| Dispensing | blocked on the MMUR question | not started |

## 10. Open questions that gate work

1. **MMUR access.** Rule 64-4.222(2)(a) assumes the MMTC can verify patient status and
   limits during a disconnection. How third-party software performs that check is not
   publicly documented. No dispensing module until this is answered.
2. **Anchor operator.** BioTrack issues API credentials only to a vendor naming the
   MMTC or CMTL it integrates for. The first customer is also the certification sponsor.
3. **Department API wire format.** The department's side of the contract is public and
   encoded here. The wire format needs a sponsor to see.
4. **Repo org handle.** Bare `coontie` is held by a GitHub user.
5. **Freedom to operate.** A patent read before anything ships.

## 11. What this spec does not claim

- That the product is certified, or that it can legally act as an MMTC's internal STS
  tomorrow. It cannot. That requires an approved integration plan and a validation
  with the department's vendor.
- That the pain research is a market study. It is a corpus of public complaints and one
  hearing record. The next tier of evidence is five operator interviews.
- That the module order is a frequency ranking of failures. It follows the licence
  calendar and the penalty structure, and that method is stated where it is used.
